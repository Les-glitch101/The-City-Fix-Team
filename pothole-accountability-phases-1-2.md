## 2. Project Objective (one paragraph)

We help Emfuleni Local Municipality (and, by extension, Vaal Triangle road users and neighbouring authorities) turn pothole reporting and road-maintenance patching from a reactive, hard-to-account-for activity into a managed, trackable, evidence-producing project — so the municipality can show residents, the mayor's office, the Gauteng MEC/COGTA, and the Provincial Legislature what was reported, what was prioritised, what was done, what remains open, and why, with dates and evidence.

This is not a promise to fix 14,700 km of roads. It is a promise to make whatever capacity exists visible, prioritised, and accountable — starting with a small, credible pilot.

---

## 3. Scope Boundary (explicit, for credibility)

**In scope (v1):**
- Standardised pothole/ road-defect intake with reference numbers (from existing municipal channels and a light resident-facing interface).
- Work-item management: priority, owner/crew, target action (temporary patch / permanent repair / inspection), target date, status lifecycle, evidence capture (photo/ notes).
- Three reporting views: resident status view; operational view (roads team / mayoral programme / MMC); accountability view (mayor's office, municipal manager, SDBIP-relevant indicators, and — when needed — COGTA/Provincial Legislature/media).
- Operationalisation of SDBIP-relevant turnaround/delivery indicators for the pilot area/ programme.
- A defined pilot on the "War on Decay" patching programme (or one ward/ area), bounded in time (e.g. 6–12 weeks) and geography.

**Out of scope (v1):**
- Full network condition surveying / PCI-IRI assessment (the pilot does not perform this; it can feed a later phase).
- Automatic road-authority routing as a primary solved problem (the Gauteng PotholeFixGP app already claims this for Gauteng provincial roads; the municipality's capacity to act remains the real barrier).
- Payment systems, procurement, or financial recovery.
- Fixing the R636m returned MIG, the unfunded budget for 9 consecutive years, or the failed financial recovery plan — these are preconditions/ context, not solvable by this system alone.

**Honest framing for stakeholders:** This system does not fix roads; it manages the work of fixing them. The roads budget, crews, materials, and procurement do that. What the system does is make that work visible, prioritised, trackable, and accountable.

## 6. As-Is Workflow Diagram (Emfuleni, text version + confidence tags)

### 6.1 Core flow (what exists today, with confidence tags)

**Legend:** [H] = high confidence (well-sourced); [M] = medium confidence (reasonably supported, some inference); [L] = low confidence (not directly confirmed; flag for verification).

1. **Resident observes pothole** [H]
2. **Resident attempts to report** — channels: website "Log a Service Fault" [H]; Customer Care phone 016 950 5109 / 087 310 8348 [H]; email customercare@emfuleni.gov.za [H]; possibly walk-in at Vereeniging/ Vanderbijlpark offices [M].
3. **Intake:** report enters a general fault-intake path; **no dedicated pothole channel and no auto-generated public reference number confirmed** [M — the website form's reference-number behaviour was not fully extractable; treat as an open question].
4. **Prioritisation (opaque):** no published prioritisation rule or network-condition data; activity is campaign/ programme-driven ("War on Decay," Service Delivery Fridays, Jetpatcher deployments) [M].
5. **Execution (if it happens):** patching, sometimes via Jetpatcher; temporary vs permanent not consistently distinguished or published [M].
6. **Verification/ tracking (weak/ publicly absent):** no public pothole dashboard; no published closure rate; no published list of reported-and-pending potholes; SDBIP measures internal turnaround and supplier-payment turnaround, not resident-facing repair outcomes [H for publicly absent; M for internal SDBIP indicator interpretation].
7. **Resident feedback loop (weak):** resident may or may not get a reference number or status update; common reported outcome is "no response" [H — from user-story evidence].
8. **Escalation (if frustrated):** ward councillor; municipal manager; mayor's office/ MMC; Gauteng COGTA/ MEC; Provincial Legislature (written questions); media; PAIA; Section 139/ SAHRC [H for existence of routes; M for how often they're used in practice].

### 6.2 Silent-failure branches (likely, with confidence)

- **Report logged, no record retrievable by resident** — because no reference number/ tracking is confirmed; [M].
- **Report logged, no prioritisation, no crew dispatch** — because 70% of 14,700 km needs full maintenance and capacity/ budget is constrained; [H for constraint; M for per-report outcome].
- **Report routed wrongly (authority ambiguity)** — resident may not know if road is municipal/ provincial/ national; Gauteng PotholeFixGP claims to route, but routing doesn't create municipal capacity; [M].
- **Response is "we're patching here and there" without a trackable record** — campaign-style patching without a dashboard; [M].
- **Municipal staff unreachable** — documented: the municipality failed to provide comment to press and a governance expert; [H].

### 6.3 The gap the project targets (text)

The gap is not "no reporting channel exists." The gap is: **no consistent intake record with reference number + no prioritisation rule + no work-item management + no verification/ evidence + no public status view + no accountability view — all against a network in crisis and a financial/ governance backdrop that undermines delivery.** A PM-flavoured system closes the operational-visibility and accountability gap.

## 8. Pain Points + Opportunities

### 8.1 Pain points (source-backed)

1. **No consistent intake record with reference number.** [M] — website/ Customer Care exist but no dedicated channel or confirmed auto reference number.
2. **No published public repair SLA / response-time commitment.** [H — publicly absent; cf. Cape Town, Midvaal].
3. **No public status view for residents.** [H — no public dashboard/ closure rate].
4. **No operational work-item management (priority/ owner/ target date/ evidence).** [M — inferred from campaign-style patching + SDBIP indicator language].
5. **No accountability view for mayor's office/ SDBIP/ COGTA/ Legislature.** [M — inferred from weak public evidence + SDBIP being internal].
6. **Network in crisis + constrained/ mishandled funding.** [H — 70% of 14,700 km; R500m; R289m; R636m returned MIG; unfunded budget x9 years; failed recovery plan].
7. **Routing ambiguity (which authority owns the road).** [M — boundary split + PotholeFixGP routing claim].
8. **Municipal silence/ unreachable staff.** [H — South Africa Today, 13 April 2026].
9. **Safety hazard with an extreme, unverified allegation.** [M for hazard; L for the "4 deaths" claim — verify].

### 8.2 Opportunities (what the project can do)

1. **Standardised intake + reference numbers** across existing channels.
2. **Resident-facing status view** (reference number → status), reducing "no response" complaints.
3. **Work-item management** (priority/ owner/ target date/ status/ evidence) for the roads team / mayoral programme.
4. **Operational view** for the MMC/ mayor's office (what's open, dispatched, completed, pending by area/ crew/ priority).
5. **Accountability view** for SDBIP-relevant indicators + mayor's office + (when needed) COGTA/ Legislature/ media.
6. **Pilot on the "War on Decay" programme** (or one ward/ area) — small, credible, accountable.
7. **Frequent-road-user data source** (drivers/ ride-share/ minibus-taxi-adjacent) to build a baseline condition picture and pressure-point evidence.
8. **Parallel provincial route (PotholeFixGP)** as comparison/ backup when the municipal channel is silent.

## 10. Requirements Specification

### 10.1 Functional requirements (FRs)

| ID | Requirement | Source/ rationale |
|---|---|---|
| FR-01 | The system shall accept pothole/ road-defect reports from the municipality's existing intake channels (website "Log a Service Fault" and Customer Care) and create a single consistent record. | Emfuleni channels (Section 9.1); pain point 1. |
| FR-02 | The system shall issue a reference number for each reported pothole/ defect and allow the reporter to retrieve status by that reference number. | Pain point 1 + 3; persona Sipho/ Nomsa. |
| FR-03 | The system shall capture for each report: location (address/ landmark/ GPS pin), description, severity (working definition — see urgent-pothole doc), photo (optional), date/ time, and road-authority guess (municipal/ provincial/ national). | Pain point 7; FR-01; personas. |
| FR-04 | The system shall support work-item management: priority, owner/ crew, target action (temporary patch / permanent repair / inspection), target date, status lifecycle, and evidence capture (photo/ notes). | Pain point 4; personas Thabo; SDBIP operationalisation. |
| FR-05 | The system shall support prioritisation by definable rules (severity, traffic, safety, area, programme) that can be configured and explained. | Pain point 4; urgent-pothole doc; Thabo persona. |
| FR-06 | The system shall provide a resident-facing status view (reference number → status) accessible without requiring the resident to call the municipality. | Pain point 3; personas Sipho/ Nomsa/ Lerato. |
| FR-07 | The system shall provide an operational view for the roads team/ mayoral programme/ MMC showing open, dispatched, in-progress, completed/ verified, and pending items by area, crew, and priority. | Pain point 5; persona Thabo. |
| FR-08 | The system shall provide an accountability view for the mayor's office/ municipal manager/ SDBIP performance reporting, and — when needed — COGTA/ Provincial Legislature/ media, showing throughput, closure rate, turnaround, area coverage, and evidence. | Pain point 5; Section 4.6 SDBIP; escalation routes. |
| FR-09 | The system shall operationalise SDBIP-relevant turnaround/ delivery indicators for the pilot area/ programme (e.g. turnaround time for reports; closure rate; time from logged to dispatched). | Section 4.6 SDBIP; pain point 4. |
| FR-10 | The system shall allow a parallel report route via the Gauteng PotholeFixGP app as a comparison/ backup channel for Gauteng provincial roads, with note that routing does not create municipal capacity. | Section 5.3; pain point 7. |
| FR-11 | The system shall be low-bandwidth and mobile-friendly for crews on the ground (capture via phone, weak data tolerant). | Persona Thabo/ Lerato; operational realism. |
| FR-12 | The system shall support a defined pilot scope (one programme/ area/ ward, bounded in time) with clear in/out-of-scope and success measures. | Section 3; PM pitch. |

### 10.2 Non-functional requirements (NFRs)

| ID | Requirement | Source/ rationale |
|---|---|---|
| NFR-01 | Accessibility: the resident-facing view shall be accessible to residents with limited data/ low-end devices; minimal friction to report and check status. | Persona Sipho/ Lerato; context. |
| NFR-02 | Reliability of record: a reported pothole shall not disappear from the system; the record persists and is retrievable by reference number. | Pain point 1 (silent-failure branch). |
| NFR-03 | Transparency: statuses and actions shall be explainable (what happened, when, by whom, with what evidence) in plain language for residents and for accountability audiences. | Pain point 5; accountability view. |
| NFR-04 | Privacy/ data handling: location data and reporter info handled per applicable norms; no over-collection; avoid publishing sensitive personal info. | General NFR; to be confirmed with stakeholders. |
| NFR-05 | Operability under constraint: the system must function even when higher-level municipal staff are unreachable — i.e., it must support ward-level/ roads-team/ community pilot operation without depending on full municipal engagement. | Section 6.2 silent-failure branches; Section 8 opportunity 8; persona Nomsa. |
| NFR-06 | Scalability-in-perspective: the pilot should be architected so a later phase could extend to more areas/ programmes/ indicators without a full rebuild (but the pilot does not promise province-wide road AI). | Section 3 scope boundary. |
| NFR-07 | Verifiability: the system shall produce evidence (photos/ notes/ timestamps/ status history) sufficient for PAIA-style queries and accountability reporting. | Pain point 5; Section 9.7 PAIA. |

### 10.3 User stories (with acceptance criteria)

**US-1 — Thabo logs and routes a reported pothole (Dispatcher)**
- *As a* roads-team dispatcher/ coordinator, *I want to* receive a reported pothole as a single work item with a reference number, location, severity, and photo, *so that* I can prioritise it, assign a crew, and track it to completion.
- *Acceptance:*
  - A report submitted via the website/ Customer Care becomes one consistent record with a reference number.
  - Thabo can set priority, owner/crew, target action, target date, and status.
  - Thabo can capture evidence (photo/ notes) when the crew acts.
  - Thabo can see open/ dispatched/ completed/ pending items by area and crew.
  - If municipal staff are unreachable, the system still supports Thabo (or a proxy) operating the pilot record.

**US-2 — Nomsa gives a resident an answer (Ward Councillor)**
- *As a* ward councillor, *I want to* look up a resident's reported pothole by reference number and see its status, *so that* I can tell the resident what's happening instead of "I'll check."
- *Acceptance:*
  - Nomsa can retrieve a report by reference number.
  - Nomsa can see current status and recent activity/ evidence.
  - Nomsa has an area/ ward view of what's been logged and done in her ward.
  - If the municipality is silent, Nomsa can use the pilot record as the basis for escalation to the municipal manager/ mayor's office/ MMC.

**US-3 — Sipho reports and checks without calling (Commuter)**
- *As a* private commuter, *I want to* report a pothole with location/ photo/ a reference number and check its status online, *so that* I'm not left in the dark and I have something to escalate with if nothing happens.
- *Acceptance:*
  - Sipho can submit a report (location/ description/ photo/ road-authority guess) and receive/ keep a reference number.
  - Sipho can check status by reference number without calling the municipality.
  - If the pothole is on/ near a Gauteng provincial road, Sipho is told PotholeFixGP is an alternative route (with the caveat that routing doesn't create capacity).
  - A "still open/ pending" status is visible and better than invisibility.

**US-4 — Lerato logs defects on her route (Ride-share/ frequent road user)**
- *As a* frequent road user (ride-share/ minibus-taxi-adjacent driver), *I want to* quickly log defects I see repeatedly on my route with location/ photo/ reference number, *so that* the roads I depend on are visible and I can see whether they're being worked on.
- *Acceptance:*
  - Lerato can log defects with low effort (location/ photo/ reference number).
  - Lerato can see status where available.
  - Repeated defects on a route can be surfaced (baseline condition picture) as a phase-2 opportunity.
  - Lerato's reports are treated as data/ stakeholder input, not as a replacement for municipal action.

**US-5 — Mayor's office/ municipal manager shows the record (Accountability)**
- *As a* mayor's office/ municipal manager/ SDBIP reporter, *I want to* produce a view showing what was reported, prioritised, dispatched, completed, and pending — with dates and evidence — *so that* I can answer residents, the MEC/ COGTA, the Provincial Legislature, and the media with a record instead of improvising.
- *Acceptance:*
  - The accountability view can be generated for a defined period/ area/ programme.
  - It shows throughput, closure rate, turnaround, area coverage, and evidence.
  - It operationalises SDBIP-relevant indicators for the pilot.
  - It is PAIA-able (records retrievable on request).

**Team:** The City Fix Team

**Mission:** Help Emfuleni (and Vaal Triangle road users/ neighbouring authorities) turn pothole reporting and road-maintenance patching into a managed, trackable, evidence-producing project — starting with a small, credible pilot on the "War on Decay" patching programme (or one ward/ area), bounded in time and geography.

## 14. Phase 1 Deliverable — Urgent-Pothole Definition Doc (Draft v1, to be refined with stakeholders)

### 14.1 Working definition

An **urgent pothole** is a pothole/ road defect that presents an immediate or near-immediate risk to safety, vehicle integrity, or access, and that should be prioritised for inspection/ temporary patching (at minimum) ahead of routine work.

### 14.2 Suggested triage signals (draft — refine with Thabo/ ward/ municipality)

**Safety signals (strong):**
- Pothole large/ deep enough to risk tyre/ rim/ suspension/ body damage, or to destabilise a vehicle at speed.
- Pothole in a location that forces traffic into danger (e.g. near a junction, pedestrian crossing, school route, taxi rank approach, emergency-vehicle route).
- Pothole associated with (or alleged to be associated with) a crash/ injury — **if the "4 deaths" claim is verified, this is the highest-priority category; until verified, treat as an allegation warranting investigation.**

**Access/ network signals (medium):**
- Pothole on a road serving essential access (e.g. emergency services, water/ sewer response, refuse collection, school access).
- Pothole contributing to drainage/ waterlogging that threatens further road degradation (cf. Midvaal's stormwater-first approach after heavy rain).

**Programme signals (contextual):**
- Pothole falls within the "War on Decay" patching programme area/ priority area (Vereeniging/ Vanderbijlpark/ Sebokeng/ Evaton/ Three Rivers/ Sharpeville — the areas Emfuleni itself named as worst-affected).
- Pothole is part of a cluster/ recurring spot that indicates a deeper defect (cf. AA's point that reactive patching with weak materials worsens recurrence).

**Exclusion/ caveat:**
- "Urgent" is about triage and prioritisation, not a guarantee of same-day permanent repair — capacity and budget constrain outcomes. The system should record urgency and the response, not promise an outcome it can't deliver.

### 14.3 Verification note (explicit)
The ERPA-group claim "Pothole still there that killed 4 people" is **not verified**. It is included in the research and personas as a serious allegation that warrants investigation. Any use of it as an established fact in the project requires verification first (RAF/ police/ local media/ coroner/ road-accident record). Until verified, the project treats it as: *a resident allegation of a fatal pothole-related crash and a pothole left unfixed — to be investigated, not asserted.*

### 20.4 Thematic read of the 11 interviews (Day 2 primary evidence)

**T1 — The reporting barrier is knowledge + efficacy, not absence of channels.**
- The single most repeated reason for not reporting is **"I don't know who to contact (JRA, SANRAL, or Metro)"** (IDs 2, 3, 11; and it is the survey's Q5 option that matches the dominant non-reporting reason in the group).
- The second cluster is **"No, because I feel it makes no difference"** (IDs 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10) and **"The reporting process is too complicated or takes too long"** (ID 1). One respondent said "No, I didn't know how" (ID 6).
- *Implication:* This is direct behavioural evidence for pain point 1 (no consistent intake with reference number that people know about) and pain point 8 (municipal silence / non-response erodes trust). It also validates the project's core claim: the problem is not "no reporting mechanism exists" but "reporting is confusing, feels futile, and doesn't give people visibility."

**T2 — Reported experience is thin and frustrating, even where people tried.**
- Of the 11, **3 reported once or twice** (IDs 2, 3, 11) and **1 reported despite the process being "too complicated or takes too long"** (ID 1). None in the readable set reported "frequently."
- The dominant non-reporting stance is cynicism/efficacy failure ("makes no difference"), not ignorance of roads — people know the roads are bad (ratings 1–5 span the full range, with 4–5 appearing alongside "makes no difference").
- *Implication:* Supports the resident-facing status view (FR-06) and reference-number + notification design (FR-02, FR-11) as the trust-restoring mechanism. This also aligns with the CITY FIX TEAM doc's point that "a reporting system becomes less useful if residents submit something and then hear nothing," and with the 2026 report it cites of a resident who received a reference number but experienced a long wait.

**T3 — Damage is common and ratings are harsh.**
- **Most respondents reported vehicle damage** (Yes in the large majority of readable rows; the remainder are "close calls").
- Ratings span **1 to 5**, with **low ratings (1–2)** clustering among daily drivers who also report "makes no difference" — i.e., the people most exposed are also the most disillusioned.
- *Implication:* This is direct local corroboration of the AA/Santam/RoadScout damage figures in the baseline (Section 5.4/ 8.1), but now from 11 Emfuleni-area drivers on a specific date. Useful as primary evidence in the case file.

**T4 — GPS + photo + status visibility are what people want.**
- On the app-likelihood question (Q6), the readable rows skew heavily to **"Very likely"** for a fast (under-30-second) photo + auto-GPS reporting experience.
- On the most-valued tracking feature (Q7), the readable answers split across **reference ticket number to hold the municipality accountable**, **live map of already-reported nearby hazards**, and **proof of work after repair-team photos** — i.e., the three accountability/ visibility features the project is building.
- On media preference (Q8), **"Both photo and video"** dominates the readable rows, with one "Photo only."
- *Implication:* Strongly validates FR-03 (location + photo), FR-06 (status view), and the evidence-capture design. Also validates the CITY FIX TEAM doc's argument that GPS is not a "cool app feature" but a response to a documented information problem (no GPS on pothole listings in the Annual Report).

**T5 — Sample is small and young; treat as qualitative, not representative.**
- The readable rows are **predominantly male, 18–24**, daily drivers in Emfuleni. That's a useful qualitative slice (the team's own risk register anticipates 5–10 drivers and allows for fewer), but it is **not statistically representative of all Emfuleni residents**.
- *Implication:* Keep the CITY FIX TEAM limitation language (Section 20.7 below) intact: this is qualitative insight to triangulate with secondary research, not a claim about the whole population.

### 20.5 Researcher positioning doc — CITY FIX TEAM.docx (Crystal Charles)

*Source:* CITY FIX TEAM.docx (team document, Day 2), author Crystal Charles, role stated as requirements gatherer/ documenter. The doc is structured as a Q&A positioning/ defence document for the project.

#### 20.5.1 Project framing (core)
- **Project:** a Pothole Reporting and Repair Tracking System focused on Emfuleni Local Municipality.
- **Core idea:** a system where a pothole can be reported with location, photograph, and description; the system then organises reports by detecting possible duplicates, assigning a priority level, displaying outstanding reports on a staff dashboard, and allowing status tracking.
- **Emphasis:** "not only building a form where someone submits a pothole" — the project looks at the **full process from reporting through to repair**, answering: Where exactly is the pothole? Has somebody already reported it? How serious/ urgent is it? Which reports first? Current status? Has it actually been repaired? Can the work be supported by evidence? Can management see the outstanding workload?
- **Five main features:** reporting, duplicate matching, priority classification, dashboard, and notifications.

#### 20.5.2 Why Emfuleni (municipally-sourced evidence cited)
- **2024 Status Quo Assessment (municipal source)** identifies: inadequate materials, tools and fleet to respond to complaints; lack of comprehensive maintenance; outdated ICT systems supporting service provision; high vacancies among technical staff and management; inadequate operational maintenance.
- **2024/25 IDP:** contains a performance indicator relating to the number of potholes patched on municipal roads and streets; lists a **target of 5,000 potholes patched** and identifies **Public Works and a maintenance plan** as part of the performance framework.
- **2024/25 Annual Report:** Roads Department patched **5,779.66 m² of asphalted surfaces** during the financial period; notes that this represented an **underachievement when viewed against the escalating deterioration of the municipal tarred-road network**; states that **the municipality's financial resources for tarred-road maintenance are insufficient to comprehensively address the deterioration**.
- *Cross-reference:* these municipal figures complement the Sowetan R500m / 70% of 14,700 km / R289m figures in Section 4.3.

#### 20.5.3 Existing initiatives acknowledged (positioning)
- **Operation Vala Zonke launched in 2022**, with the launch taking place on the **R57 in Emfuleni**. The initiative included a pothole-reporting app allowing the public to report potholes, upload photographs, provide location information, and receive status information through a ticketing system.
- Therefore the project does **not** claim to be the first pothole-reporting system. It asks: *What additional information-management and municipal workflow capabilities could help improve the reporting-to-repair process?* (duplicate detection; prioritisation; municipal backlog dashboard; structured status management; repair evidence; reporting and analytics.)
- *Cross-reference:* matches baseline Section 5.4 (SANRAL/ Vala Zonke) and reinforces the correct scope framing in Section 3.

#### 20.5.4 As-is workflow + confidence tags (aligns with Section 6)
- **As-is (high-level):** (1) Resident identifies pothole; (2) Resident reports a service problem; (3) Complaint/report is logged; (4) Report/reference information is generated where applicable; (5) Relevant municipal function responds; (6) Maintenance work is planned/ prioritised; (7) Pothole is repaired. Internal details deliberately marked **Confirmed / Assumed / Unknown** because the team does **not** have municipal staff access.
- **Things the team explicitly does NOT claim to know:** that every pothole gets GPS coordinates; that every pothole receives a unique digital ID; that every duplicate is automatically merged; that every resident receives live status updates; that every repaired pothole has before-and-after photographs.
- *Cross-reference:* this is consistent with and sharpens our Section 6.1/ 6.2 confidence tags.

#### 20.5.5 Strongest evidence piece (GPS + verification)
- From **Emfuleni's 2024/25 Annual Report**: the report discusses a performance indicator for potholes patched and states that **the reported performance could not be fully verified**. Audit-related findings:
  - pothole listings did not have GPS coordinates;
  - planned potholes also lacked specific GPS references;
  - job cards did not contain length and breadth information needed to verify the reported square metres;
  - the reported patched area therefore could not be verified;
  - the municipality did not ensure appropriate performance systems for verifying reported achievements.
- **Remedial action:** adding a reference point to job cards to provide additional location information.
- *Implication:* This is a direct, municipally-documented justification for location capture (FR-03), structured records, status history/ audit trail (NFR-07), repair evidence (FR-04/ FR-12), and the accountability view (FR-08). Arguably the single strongest evidence anchor in the whole case.

#### 20.5.6 Priority classification model (decision-support, not autonomous)
- **Three initial factors:** road type; traffic volume; pothole size.
- The system calculates a **proposed priority score** and ranks reports; it is a **proposed academic classification model, not an official Emfuleni municipal prioritisation formula**.
- **Final priority remains a human/ municipal decision**, because staff may weigh factors the system doesn't know: available resources; road closures; existing maintenance schedules; emergency work; weather; contractor availability; other municipal priorities.
- *Cross-reference:* aligns with FR-05 and our triage signals in Section 14.2; reinforces the honest framing that the system doesn't fix roads or promise to repair everything.

#### 20.5.7 Staff dashboard + backlog map + status workflow
- **Dashboard shows:** total reports; unresolved reports; priority levels; current status; location; report age; map position; possible duplicates.
- **Backlog map:** Leaflet/ OpenStreetMap (project plan assigns Crystal and Raymond to the staff dashboard and backlog map).
- **Proposed status workflow:** Reported → Assigned → In Progress → Repaired (additional states possible).
- **Notifications:** received → assigned → in progress → marked repaired.
- *Cross-reference:* aligns with FR-04, FR-07, FR-08, FR-11.

#### 20.5.8 FR/NFR set (team's own)
- **FR1–FR12:** Submit a pothole report; capture location; upload evidence (photo); generate a report identifier; track status; detect possible duplicates; classify priority; staff dashboard; map reports; update status; notifications; record repair information.
- **NFRs:** usability (simple submission); performance (reasonable response time); reliability (submitted report should not disappear); security (protect user info/ access); accessibility (understandable/ usable by different users); maintainability (structured for improvement).
- *Cross-reference:* maps closely onto our Section 10.1/ 10.2.

#### 20.5.9 Testing approach + limitations
- **Triage experiment:** take the same set of sample pothole reports and perform (Method A) manual prioritisation vs (Method B) the system's priority classification; compare rankings, consistency, and time. This demonstrates whether the proposed prioritisation works as a prototype — **not** that it would improve Emfuleni's actual operations (which would require municipal staff access).
- **What can be tested:** technical functionality; usability; duplicate matching; priority classification; API behaviour; dashboard behaviour.
- **What cannot be fully validated without municipal staff:** "This is exactly how Emfuleni staff would use the system in their daily operations."
- **Limitations explicitly documented:** (1) no municipal staff access; (2) public-information limits; (3) small driver sample (5–10, not statistically representative); (4) evidence-based personas (staff personas from public evidence, not direct staff interviews); (5) academic prototype (not automatically an official Emfuleni system); (6) existing initiatives (Vala Zonke) requiring careful positioning; scope creep risk.
- *Cross-reference:* matches our risk register R-01/ R-02/ R-09 and our persona limitation language.

#### 20.5.10 Problem statement + research question (useful for charter/pitch)
- **Problem statement:** Emfuleni Local Municipality faces ongoing road-maintenance and pothole challenges within a wider environment of resource, maintenance and ICT constraints. Although pothole-reporting mechanisms and maintenance activities exist, there are opportunities to improve the structured management of pothole reports through location capture, duplicate detection, priority classification, status tracking, dashboard visibility and repair information. The proposed system therefore aims to support the reporting-to-repair workflow by providing better organised information for residents and municipal staff.
- **Research question:** How can a digital pothole reporting and repair tracking system improve the organisation, prioritisation, visibility and tracking of pothole reports within Emfuleni Local Municipality?
- **Sub-questions:** How are potholes currently reported? What problems do road users experience when reporting? What information is needed to manage reports effectively? How can duplicate reports be identified? How can potholes be prioritised? How can residents track reports? How can municipal staff view/ manage the backlog? How can repair information be recorded and verified? How can the proposed system be evaluated?
- *Cross-reference:* these are good anchors for the charter mission (Section 11) and the pitch.
